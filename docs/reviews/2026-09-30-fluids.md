# Fluids review: grid solvers, analytic helpers, thin film

2026-09-30 · base `37cf53f`

This reviews the fluid simulations the September 29 review had not covered:

- the Eulerian grid solvers `FluidGrid` and `FluidGrid3D`
- the analytic helpers (drag, buoyancy, Reynolds number, `Fluid` presets)
- the `thin_film` lubrication law

`SphFluid` and particle coupling were covered in `2026-09-29-correctness-performance.md` and are not repeated here. The river solver that grew out of this review has its own report, `2026-09-30-river-solver.md`, and its own pull request.

Every finding was checked against an independent oracle, not against the code's own output. The oracles:

- analytic diffusion and Taylor–Green decay rates, and a projection's theoretical floor
- conservation laws and brute-force sums over fluid cells
- a brute-force integral of the Bingham velocity profile
- Huppert's similarity solutions
- Stokes' law and Hagen–Poiseuille
- IAPWS, ITTC and ICAO property tables

Every finding is fixed, and each keeps the test that failed on the base as a regression test. Seven were first left open for a decision; they were fixed in a second pass, described with each one below.

## Summary

| Area | Findings | Fixed | Open |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Grid solvers (`FluidGrid`, `FluidGrid3D`) | 13 | 13 | 0 |
| Analytic helpers and presets | 4 | 4 | 0 |
| Thin film | 2 | 2 | 0 |

The three things most likely to have hurt someone:

1. **3D viscosity and diffusion were N× too strong on an N³ grid** (GRID-1). They were also resolution-dependent: 9.9× at 10³ and 33.9× at 34³. The `fluid_3d` demo's viscosity was acting 24× stronger than its value. *Fixed.*
2. **The thin-film step limit ignored the levelling term** (FILM-1). On level or gentle ground it was up to 3000× too large. At a quarter of it, a smooth 2 mm film grew a ±15% checkerboard that the positivity limiter then held in place for good. *Fixed:* `max_step` is now `min(dx/c, dx²/(4D))`.
3. **2D non-square grids were wrong in both diffusion and projection** (GRID-2, GRID-3). The projection left 8.8e-2 of a pure gradient field on 34×18, against a floor of 4.4e-9, and the shipped `fluid_2d` demo is 80×60. *Fixed.*

## Grid solvers (`fluid_simulation.rs`, `fluid_simulation_3d.rs`)

**The convention everything is judged against:** length is measured in domain widths on every axis. The cell size is `1/width`, velocity is in widths/s, and viscosity and diffusion are in widths²/s. `advect`, the 3D `project` and particle coupling already used this; the defects are the places that did not.

| ID | Sev | Finding | Status | Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| GRID-1 | High | 3D viscosity and diffusion used `width·height·depth` where `width²` belongs: N× too strong and resolution-dependent (9.9× at 10³, 17.9× at 18³, 33.9× at 34³). | **Fixed** | `review_3d_diffusion_matches_the_analytic_rate_at_any_resolution_and_axis`, `review_3d_viscosity_matches_taylor_green_decay` |
| GRID-2 | Medium | 2D diffusion used `width·height`, so it was right only on square grids: 34×18 decayed at 0.53× the analytic rate, 34×66 at 1.94×. | **Fixed** | `review_2d_diffusion_rate_does_not_depend_on_the_aspect_ratio` |
| GRID-3 | High | The 2D projection scaled the y pressure gradient by `height`. On non-square grids it left 8.8e-2 of a pure gradient field (floor 4.4e-9). | **Fixed** | `review_2d_projection_removes_a_gradient_field_on_non_square_grids` |
| GRID-4 | Medium | `get_total_mass` and `get_kinetic_energy` summed the ghost ring. 1.0 of dye diffused on 10×10 reported 1.5625 (2.37 on 8³); kinetic energy was 25% over in 2D and 41% in 3D. | **Fixed** | `review_total_mass_is_conserved_by_pure_diffusion`, `review_kinetic_energy_counts_each_fluid_cell_once` |

### What the pressure solve costs (GRID-9)

`SolverConfig::pressure_tolerance` (a relative residual) and `pressure_max_iterations` (default 200 per projection) set the trade. Each 128² row below is one step of a forced plume, as a game drives it, timed with criterion (`benches/fluid_grid.rs`, group `pressure_tolerance`). *Plume after 2 s* is the velocity field's distance from a converged solve after 2 s at 130². *Energy left* is what one cold step leaves of a smooth divergence.

| Tolerance | 2D 128² step | Plume after 2 s | Energy left |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| old: 4 relaxation sweeps | 3.72 ms | 91% off | 99% |
| 0.3 (about 1e-4 of the energy) | 2.84 ms (−24%) | 5.0% off | 1e-4 |
| **1e-2 (default)** | **3.27 ms (−12%)** | **0.48% off** | **2e-9** |
| 1e-4 | 6.38 ms (+72%) | 0.004% off | 2e-13 |
| 1e-8 (converged) | 20.1 ms (+440%) | — | rounding |

The same pattern holds at 256² (0.3: −22%; default: −8%; 1e-4: +63%) and 3D 64³ (−18%, −7%, +44%).

The first fix set the default at 1e-4, which cost up to 70% more per step than the old solver for a difference nobody can see. The default is now 1e-2: the flow stops changing visibly there, and the step is cheaper than the solver it replaced. Against the pre-review code, a default step now costs:

| Grid | Before | Now |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2D 64² | 845 µs | 708 µs (−16%) |
| 2D 128² | 3.66 ms | 3.15 ms (−14%) |
| 2D 256² | 15.7 ms | 14.9 ms (−5%) |
| 3D 32³ | 8.56 ms | 7.65 ms (−11%) |
| 3D 64³ | 89.0 ms | 85.6 ms (−4%) |

- **Iterations:** under a steady source a warm projection takes 1–4 iterations at 64², up to 14 at 128², up to 22 at 256², and 3–10 in 3D. A cold, impulsive step at 130² takes 54. `get_last_pressure_iterations()` reports the count, and the cap bounds the worst case at 2 × 200.
- **Old behaviour:** `SolverConfig::default().with_pressure_solver(PressureSolver::Relaxation)` reproduces the old solver bit for bit, cost included.
- **Why MIC(0):** plain CG took 5–7× the iterations. The preconditioner's smallest pivot in the closed box is 0.59 in 2D and 1.15 in 3D at every size tested, so its safeguard never fires.
- **Determinism:** the solve is serial, with a fixed 8-lane order for dot products, so results do not depend on the thread count.

**What holds up:**

- On square grids, 2D viscosity matches Taylor–Green decay (error 3e-6 at 66²).
- The projection reaches the collocated scheme's floor exactly (3.4e-11 at 34²).
- A mirror-symmetric setup stays symmetric to 1.9e-16.
- Advection units converge toward Ω·t as the grid refines.
- There are no per-call coefficients.
- The state stays finite and kinetic energy never rises at `dt = 1/30` with flow crossing about 30 cells per step.

### What FILM-1 costs a caller: steps per frame

A limit up to 3000× smaller does not mean 3000× more steps. What a caller pays is steps per frame. This is a 128² grid of level ground, blood, stepped at 60 Hz for 2 s at a quarter of `max_step`, first with the old wave-only limit and then with the new one:

| Case | Old: mean (peak) | New: mean (peak) |
|---|---:|---:|
| 2 mm film with a 1% ripple, 5 cm cells | 1 (1) | 1 (1) |
| 3 mm spill, 5 cm cells | 1.0 (2) | 1.7 (3) |
| 3 mm spill, 2 cm cells | 1.1 (12) | 3.5 (16) |
| 3 mm spill, 1 cm cells | 1.5 (47) | 5.7 (63) |

The diffusive limit goes as `dx²/h³`, so the cost appears only for deep films on fine cells. At 5 cm cells a 60 Hz frame is shorter than either limit except at a fresh spill's edge. For the 5 cm spill, yield-stress (Bingham) blood takes the same steps as Newtonian. At about 96 µs a step at 128², that spill costs 0.16 ms a frame on average, against 0.10 ms at the old limit.

### Where FILM-2's cost lands

Per face, the inclined law adds, in this order:

- the bed gradient `fall·(1/dx)`: 1 multiply
- `sec²θ = 1 + s²`: 1 multiply and 1 add
- `cos²θ = 1/sec²θ`: 1 divide
- `cos⁴θ`, the inclined drive `reach·cos²θ`, and the gain: 3 multiplies

Criterion, this repo's `+avx` build, measured three ways: before FILM-2, after it, and after it with the divide replaced by a constant.

| Benchmark | Before | After | After, no divide |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `step` 128² (Newtonian) | 81.2 µs | 95.6 µs | 91.9 µs |
| `bingham_batch` | 76.5 µs | 130.6 µs | 104.0 µs |
| `newtonian_batch` | 71.5 µs | 72.8 µs | 70.5 µs |
| `step` 1400 × 1000 | 17.5 ms | 17.1 ms | 17.0 ms |
| `max_step` 1400 × 1000 | 7.90 ms | 8.70 ms | 8.71 ms |

So at 128², the 14.4 µs regression is 3.7 µs of divide and about 10.7 µs of the five extra multiply/adds, roughly one cycle per face. Bingham pays twice: half its regression is the divide, and the other half is that its own divide, `X = yield/drive`, now waits on the `cos²θ` chain. At map size the step is memory-bound, and the extra arithmetic is hidden.

The inclination depends only on the bed, so it could be cached per face when the ground changes. That removes the divide and three of the multiplies from the step, but adds 16 bytes per cell of reads, a loss at map size where the step is memory-bound. So it is not done here.

Two approximations remain, both documented on `flux_on_bed`:

- On a grade, the levelling term is still overstated by `1 + s²`, which is +4% at 0.2.
- A bed inclined diagonally to the grid is overstated by up to +4% at a grade of 0.2, against +8% before.

The `thin_film` module docs carry a marked *Changed 2026-09-30 (FILM-2)* section with the per-face change, for any transcription of the old law. Nothing in Ridgeline consumes `thin_film` or the grid solvers; its stain uses `puddle_depth`, which is unchanged.

**What holds up:**

- The Bingham plug factor matches a brute-force profile integral to 1e-10.
- The film solver reproduces Huppert's t^(1/5) pool and t^(1/3) slope current at first order.
- Stokes drag and Hagen–Poiseuille hold to 1e-12.
- Water, seawater and blood match IAPWS, ITTC and the Casson fit.
- `Fluid::from_air(&Air::sea_level())` matches ICAO.

**One constant changed:** `Fluid::glycerin().viscosity`, from 1.5 to 1.412 Pa·s (FLD-4). No material, water, air or blood constant changed.

## Behaviour changes to expect

- **3D grids:** viscosity and diffusion are N× weaker for the same numbers, 24× in the `fluid_3d` demo. The demo keeps its authored `viscosity: 0.1`, which now means what it says, so it looks much less syrupy.
- **Non-square 2D grids:** diffusion changes by W/H, and the projection actually removes divergence, so the 80×60 `fluid_2d` demo flows differently. Square 2D grids, and 3D grids with zero viscosity and diffusion, step bit-identically to before.
- **Mass and energy readouts:** `get_total_mass` and `get_kinetic_energy` exclude the ghost ring, so their values drop.
- **Film step:** `FilmFlow::max_step` and `FilmGrid::max_step` return smaller steps on level and gentle ground, so a caller stepping at a fraction of them takes more steps there. Steep slopes are unchanged.
- **Pressure solve (GRID-9):**
  - Every grid flow with velocity changes numerically, because it is now actually incompressible, and swirls hold together better.
  - A default step costs 4–16% less than before.
  - The old solver is one config line away.
- **API (GRID-8, 10, 11):**
  - `set_solver_config` returns `Result`.
  - `SolverConfig` gains public fields (`pressure_solver`, `pressure_tolerance`, `pressure_max_iterations`, `wall`), so a struct literal needs `..Default::default()`.
  - `SolverConfig::sor` no longer panics.
  - `add_density`/`add_velocity` on the ghost ring return `Err`. The demos only write inside, and ignore the result anyway.
- **Analytic helpers (FLD-2):** drag, Reynolds number and buoyancy at zero return `Ok(0.0)` instead of `Err`.
- **Glycerin (FLD-4):** 6% less viscous.
- **Film on slopes (FILM-2):** flux, speed and wave speed drop by `cos⁴θ` (−2% at a grade of 0.1, −8% at 0.2), and a yielding film's arrest depth rises by `1 + s²`. Level ground is identical.
- **Stricter input:**
  - Grid constructors reject any dimension below 3.
  - NaN and ∞ are rejected by the grid constructors and setters, `add_density`, `add_velocity`, `Fluid::new`, every `calculate_*`, and the shared validators.

## Also noticed

The repo's `.cargo/config.toml` sets `rustflags = ["-C", "target-feature=+avx"]`, so every in-repo test and benchmark runs 4-wide. A crate that depends on `rs_physics` gets the plain SSE2 build instead, so in-repo timings understate what users pay. To reproduce a user's numbers, run with an empty `RUSTFLAGS`. The config is left alone here.
